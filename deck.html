The problem

Nobody has publicly tested these models in Spanish

AI language models are entering life-science workflows, but dual-use biosecurity evaluations are done almost entirely in English and on a few frontier models. Open-weight models from Latin America and the Caribbean, such as LatamGPT, can be downloaded, modified and run offline, so safeguards cannot be monitored or recalled after release. Nobody has publicly tested whether they give meaningful help on harmful biological queries in Spanish.

Why now

Why 100 Days Mission speed raises the stakes

Compressed vaccine timelines favor fast adoption of AI tools and leave less time for review. Biosafety and biosecurity practices vary widely where research takes place. An unevaluated model becomes a larger, harder-to-see misuse surface exactly when the response needs trust and speed.

The solution in plain language

We inspect AI models like a safety inspector tests a car

We ask a designed set of questions in Spanish and English, record where a model helps when it should refuse, compare it with well-known models, and publish the results with a short guide so developers can fix problems and regulators know what to ask for.

How it works

Four steps, fully remote

STEP 1
Expert-reviewed prompt set by risk category
STEP 2
Run on LatamGPT, other LAC open-weight models, and reference modelsMistral-7B, Llama-3-8B, GPT-4o
STEP 3
Score responses, EN and ES
STEP 4
Aggregate report + responsible disclosure to developers

Needs no wet lab, no biological materials, modest compute. Reproducible remotely anywhere.

Safeguards

Dual-use safeguards

  • Aggregate, risk-category scoring; no actionable model outputs published
  • Full prompt set and raw outputs access-controlled, shared only with vetted researchers
  • Prompts stay at risk-category level, with no pathogen-specific protocols
  • Biosecurity expert review before release
  • Responsible disclosure to developers before publication
  • Every report states that a pass is not a safety guarantee
